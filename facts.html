What counts as e‑waste?

  • Phones, tablets, laptops, computers, TVs, printers.
  • Small appliances: kettles, toasters, hair dryers, toys.
  • Batteries, cables, chargers, LED bulbs, circuit boards.
  • Large appliances: fridges, washing machines, AC units.

Hidden wealth inside devices

Electronics are far richer in metals than natural ores.

Precious metals

  • Gold, silver, platinum, palladium in PCBs and connectors.
  • 1 tonne of phones ≈ 250–350 g gold; ore ≈ 5–10 g/tonne.

Base & critical metals

  • Copper, cobalt, nickel, tin, tungsten in circuits and batteries.
  • 90% of electronics contain rare earth elements.

Why it matters

  • Recovery can be cheaper and lower‑carbon than mining.
  • Only ~1% of rare earth demand met via recycling today.
