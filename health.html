When e‑waste meets people & nature

Unsafe handling pollutes air, water, and soil, and creates serious health risks—especially for children and pregnant women.

People Planet

Human health impacts

Informal and unsafe recycling exposes communities to toxic metals and chemicals.

🧒

Children

Higher exposure through dust, soil, and hand‑to‑mouth behaviour; linked to impaired neurological and behavioural development.

🤰

Pregnant women

Prenatal heavy‑metal exposure associated with reduced birth weight/length and altered neonatal development.

Workers and nearby residents

  • Heavy metals: lead, mercury, cadmium; flame retardants; dioxins.
  • Unsanitary practices can raise disease risks in poorly managed settings.
  • Formal recycling controls emissions, captures toxins, and protects workers.

Environmental impacts

How e‑waste harms ecosystems and why responsible management helps nature.

Air

  • Open burning releases toxic fumes and particulate matter.
  • Acid leaching emits hazardous gases.

Water & soil

  • Heavy metals and chemicals leach into groundwater and soil.
  • Contamination affects crops, livestock, and drinking water.

Wildlife

  • Toxins move through food chains and harm biodiversity.
  • Recycling reduces pressure on new mining and habitat loss.

How e‑waste management helps nature

  • Reduces pollution of air, water, and soil.
  • Prevents harmful chemicals from entering the environment.
  • Conserves natural resources through material recovery.
  • Protects wildlife and ecosystems.
